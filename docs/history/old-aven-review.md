# Old Aven, seen running (27 Oct 2026)

Screens taken headless at 384 × 832, dark, with demo data (blur drawn through SwiftShader; banding and smoothness
"needs phone"). Old Aven's design is inspiration only (Omar, 27 Oct). Nothing here is carried over by default; the
"worth beating" items go to Omar as improved proposals in Checkpoint 2.

## Worth beating (strong ideas, to propose in an improved form)
- **The Coach sentence with a number pill** (Health): "Romanian Deadlift has stalled at (60 kg × 12) for 5 sessions."
  The number is a tappable pill inside a big serif sentence. Data and words in one line. Could become the Coach's
  voice everywhere (morning line, workout line).
- **"Next time" on the summary**: the finish screen ends with a concrete target ("Lat Pulldown 55 kg × 9, +2.5 kg this
  time"). Makes finishing point forward.
- **Rest bar with the next set** ("Rest · Next: Lat Pulldown 1:27") floating over the list, orb at its end.
- **Lift hint on the card** ("55 kg × 8 · your heaviest yet"): the Coach inside the workout, small and quiet.
- **Prev column** in the set row: last time's numbers at the same position, tap to copy.

## Weak (don't repeat)
- **Today is crowded and uneven**: a stalled-lift banner, a huge kcal number floating in empty space, three round
  buttons, a water pill, food chips and the workout card all compete. The one "next thing to do" isn't obvious.
- **Too many orange solids**: play buttons, ticks, Done, checkmarks all use the same loud coral; boldness is spent
  everywhere, so nothing stands out.
- **The Coach screen with no key** is a dead end: three suggestions you can't use and an "Add a key" chip.
- **Set rows are dense**: five columns of boxes per row with similar weight; the check is the only clear target.
- **Mixed type voices**: italic serif titles, upright serif headings, sans everywhere else, with no clear rule.
- **The dock** carries five things (four tabs plus the orb) and the active tab's pill shifts the layout.
- **Health's sections** read as a list of rows with small charts; strong data, but no single picture of "how am I".
