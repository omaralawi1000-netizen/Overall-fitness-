# What Omar loves in old Aven (1 Nov 2026, late night)

Omar rejected all six new looks today (Ink, Ink rich, Heat, Day line, Rooms, Ordinary). Old Aven is the only design he hasn't rejected. Seven reviewers (advocate, award juror, motion director, roaster, coroner of the six rejected looks, stranger on the gym floor, identity designer) studied old Aven with demo data, then argued for two rounds. This file is their findings, merged. **It is evidence and guesses, not decisions.** His answers to the taste test decide; only his picks go into `keepers.md`.

Evidence (shared folder, not in git): `aven2/taste-2026-11-01/` holds the brief, all 14 debate files, 48 old-Aven screenshots (`old-aven/stills/`), old-Aven films (`*-smooth.mp4` were filmed with time slowed 10x, so their motion is smooth; the others are plain headless recordings) and the round screenshots.

## The finding: one recipe, thrown away six times
All seven agree the rejected looks died of the same thing: they dropped old Aven's light. The recipe, with its source:
1. **A near-black room lit by one soft coloured glow**, off-centre, different per area (old `03-today`, `06-train-plan`, `14-food`). Not a full-screen colour field (Rooms, Heat) and not flat grey (Ink: he called grey "dead").
2. **Clear, untinted glass on top**, so the glow shows through (v0.1.130: "what I've been looking for"). Every tinted or opaque card was rejected (navy "very ugly", charcoal "ruined").
3. **Italic serif names** (Pull, Breakfast, Today) **next to thin, light sans numbers** (2,500 at weight 220). The new looks used heavy poster digits and grotesk names.
4. **One hot thing to tap**: the coral play disc. The new looks used a solid full-width bottom bar.
5. **The dotted orb**: the one object that is only his (launch screen, dock).
6. **Motion that grows from what you touched** and a real **ceremony at the finish** (medal turns, numbers roll, "Strongest Lat Pulldown yet").

The design-bar rules pushed the rounds away from this: "background colour = state" made Heat and Rooms flat floods, "no equal cards" pushed out the glass cards, "one big number" made the heavy poster type.

## What is missing for world-class (agreed after the debate)
1. **Rest is an 18 px pill at the bottom.** At arm's length, sweaty, you miss it. Everyone conceded: rest should own the screen, with big light type over the glow and glass, never a flat colour field.
2. **Today has no single answer.** The hero is "2,500 kcal left" with zeros under it, while Pull sits cut off under the dock. Today should answer "what now?" and change with the moment.
3. **Ticking a set has no body.** It is a tick and a small scale nudge. Old Aven's best screen moments (finish, Skyr detail) show what reward and clarity look like; the tick should hand its light on (to rest).
4. **Areas blink through an empty colour field** instead of one world that stays while content slides over it. No object travels between screens except the Health grow.
5. **The light has no source and never reacts.** Glow, orb and glass glow on their own. World-class: the light comes from somewhere (the orb) and answers what you do (his clips 01, 02, 21).
6. **The Coach is text, not an event.** Coach listening, a new record in the moment and the morning have no moment of their own.
7. **Craft debt:** pill and round-button soup on Today, four identical cards with tiny grey labels, stock line icons beside the hand-made orb, text cut off ("440…"), five small targets in the workout header.

**Best screens now:** Skyr food detail (`18-food-detail`: reads in 2 seconds, the only screen where data is drawn), the finish medal, the launch orb, the Health grow.

## Still disputed
- **Is the dark-plus-purple-and-orange glow his taste or the 2025–26 "AI app uniform"?** Advocate and coroner: his core. Identity and roaster: keep the dark room and the single lamp, retire the generic gradient.
- **Orb as a training ledger** (each bead a set): five of seven say it fails the 5-second test at dock size; at most a reveal when you tap the orb.
- **Serif on numbers too**, or names only (Skyr's serif 126 vs Today's thin sans 2,500).
